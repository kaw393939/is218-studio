## Publish two research sites for Plan

Following the [lecture](https://youtu.be/2pMHYTIjKjo?t=2245), publish **two distinct, human-reviewable research websites**:

1. **Career research → portfolio:** public source-based findings, audience and design decisions that guide your professional website. Keep the full career comparison and personal reflection private in Canvas.
2. **AI/programming exploration → creative project:** investigate an idea that interests you, compare technical approaches and turn the findings into a small testable plan. **A game is optional.**

Simple static documentation sites are enough; no paid tool, backend or finished application is required for these research sites. Present the existing research/project records as browsable websites rather than writing two duplicate reports. Link each site's repository and cross-link the two sites. Submit the programming research site/repository at your individual Creative Project Plan checkpoint and the career research site/repository at your Portfolio Plan checkpoint. Both are due at the existing section Plan deadline: **001: November 13, 10 a.m. Eastern; 003: November 12, 2:30 p.m. Eastern.**

### Make each site reviewable

Put a short index on the landing page so a partner can find:

- The question, intended audience and bounded project goal.
- Original sources, review dates and the specific evidence relevant to the question.
- What the sources establish, what you infer, and assumptions still to check.
- Alternatives considered, your choice and its reason or tradeoff.
- The next experiment or check, with expected results distinguished from observed results.
- How AI helped, what you verified yourself and its remaining limits.

Use the existing career source counts and engineering evidence below; this does not add a source or issue quota. Visuals and narration are optional. If you include audio, provide a transcript. You own the choices and accuracy, even when AI helps produce the site.

### Present, review and revise

Walk a partner through both sites. Ask them to trace one source through an observation, a decision and the next story or check. Open a specific GitHub issue in the affected repository when a claim, navigation path or decision needs work. Record your response, link the documentation or code change, verify the changed result and resolve the issue when the evidence supports it. [Research-review issue routine](/process/github-feedback).

## Turn research into your specification

1. **Start with an interest or question.** Identify work you want to try or an experience you want to create. You may still be exploring.
2. **Investigate original sources.** Record the exact source, review date, relevant observation and what remains unknown. Check requirements rather than copying an AI summary.
3. **Compare alternatives.** Explain why one concept, audience, design or technical approach serves your intended user better. Label assumptions and uncertain experiments.
4. **Write observable behavior.** Put user stories, success/failure checks, boundaries and decisions into the same project record. Link the evidence that informed them.
5. **Build and show your own work.** Use the specification to guide the creative application or portfolio website. Keep your portfolio, LinkedIn and GitHub consistent with your actual contribution, current capabilities and checked results.

No extra report is required. Link useful research and decisions from your existing record; keep the full career comparison and private reflection in Canvas.
