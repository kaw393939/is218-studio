# Four reviews. The same project grows.

The course homes already call for **four individual and four group reviews**. The stages below organize that work. Their dates, future rubrics and submission boxes are **planned until posted in Canvas**. They do not replace the current RPS-001 criteria.

## What each review adds

| Review | Show this in the existing record |
| --- | --- |
| **1 · Plan** | Two concepts; Dream/working promise/experiment; stack and reason; rough architecture; three to five stories/checks. Group: owners, reviewers and comparable effort. |
| **2 · First working slice** | One new useful path, independently expected success/failure tests, a responsibility boundary and an initial pattern/design experiment. Reuse the completed RPS baseline where relevant. |
| **3 · Integrated build** | The working promise connected across its parts; a useful pattern choice or refactoring; regression checks; peer observations; actual AI experiment result and a keep/change/drop decision. |
| **4 · Improve and hand off** | Real peer feedback → meaningful change → retest; reproducible setup; chosen revision. Explain architecture, tests, AI's contribution, a success/disappointment and what you would do differently. |

Compare **two** options where the decision matters: audience, workflow or presentation. A shared core with two small presentations is enough. Explain what you kept constant and why you chose one; you do not need two independent codebases or databases.

Reviews 1–4 are submission checkpoints. Both the first slice and integrated build develop the **Build** phase; the four-phase diagram is not a second set of numbered assignments.

## What to submit

For a posted assignment, follow its submission setting. The current RPS task takes **your own repository URL**, with merged work and `SPEC.md` evidence on `main`.

For planned project reviews, prepare links to the same evolving record, current revision/demo, issue/PR and relevant checks. Add a few lines: **what changed, what was checked, what is next**. Groups share their record and identify each member's work. Canvas will confirm how the group submits. No prompt transcript or new weekly essay is needed.

A Studio receipt and a project review may point to the same feature/test. One explains the focused skill; the other explains progress and the decision. Do not duplicate the report.

## How the worked examples are assessed

The [future-review examples](/examples/) use this **proposed teaching rubric**, 25 points per row. It is not a change to syllabus category weights; Canvas must publish any adopted future rubric before the work is graded.

| Criterion | What the example feedback checks |
| --- | --- |
| **Complete · 25** | The agreed working promise and stage's engineering evidence are present; unfinished required behavior is identified. |
| **Thoughtful · 25** | Imaginative purpose, two approaches and a justified architecture/pattern decision; a useful simpler design can be strong work. |
| **Checked · 25** | Independently expected automated tests, actual results/regression checks and real peer feedback → change → retest. |
| **Understood · 25** | Your contribution, code/design reasoning and AI expectation versus actual result; what you would change next time. |

Ambition is welcome. We assess the finished promise, engineering practice and evidence of learning, not feature count, framework prestige or whether AI impressed you. A failed optional experiment can show strong judgment; it does not replace a working promised journey. No extra reflection essay is required.

Small and well explained can be strong work. Do not list checks you did not run or present another member's work as your own.
