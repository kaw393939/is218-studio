## Prepare your Demo Day PowerPoint

The published Final Product assignments require a **short PowerPoint presentation supporting the live demonstration**. Use a consulting-style story: problem and audience → solution and live demo → evidence and results → lessons and next steps.

Give each slide one clear takeaway. Use screenshots or simple visuals, show your own contribution, and keep the slides brief enough to leave room for the demonstration and questions. Teams prepare one shared deck; individuals prepare their own. Canvas will confirm presentation slots, duration and delivery details; this guide adds no slide-count or separate upload requirement.

Keep final work, source and the continuing record at your Review 4 URLs. **No new upload or report** is required for the Final Product grade entries. If a URL changes, send the correction privately through Canvas Inbox. [Final Product links and review submission guidance](/course/milestones#final-products-and-demo-day).

## Prepare a short demonstration

1. Open your portfolio: state your career direction and show where the résumé, GitHub and LinkedIn links live.
2. Run the individual account → game → saved result/dashboard journey. Show account maintenance and one independently expected check.
3. Name the simulated customer and agreed workflow; run it and identify your work and a teammate's connected part.
4. Show an issue, the meaningful change and the retest.
5. Explain an architecture/pattern choice and one test that would catch a real mistake.
6. Compare an agent-coding expectation with the actual result: what succeeded or disappointed, and what would you change next time? Name a remaining limit.

Every member should be able to explain their own work and how it fits together. Have the source, spec and evidence ready to open. Show failure behavior where relevant; do not imply simulated inquiries, accounts or customers are real.

The instructor will set time slots from class size; this site does not assign a fictional student a real slot or add a signup requirement before one is announced.
