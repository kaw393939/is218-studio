# Your individual creative application

**Imagine something you are not yet sure you can build. Use AI to explore it, then deliver a small working experience you can test and explain.** Choose **any programming language, framework or engine**. RPS is a useful starting point; another simple game or a non-game application is welcome.

## Dream, working promise, experiment

| Part | Decide this |
| --- | --- |
| **Dream** | The bigger experience you would love to create, and who would enjoy or use it. |
| **Working promise** | One complete user journey you will finish this term. State the rules, states and observable result. |
| **Experiment** | One uncertain idea to try with AI. Set a stopping point, then keep, change or drop it using evidence. |

For example: **Dream:** an RPS world championship broadcast. **Promise:** a polished local first-to-two match with correct ties, scoring, ending and replay. **Experiment:** commentary that reacts to the match. A whole online league can remain a future direction.

[Discover ideas with AI and compare two directions](/projects/creative-challenge). Your ambition can be large; your promise gives you something finished to show.

## What the creative project demonstrates

- A meaningful creative experience with a working computational core, beyond decoration.
- A chosen language/framework and a reason it suits the experience. Browser, desktop, mobile, engine or server approaches can work; explain how someone runs and checks yours.
- Three to five checkable stories and a manageable A/B design comparison. Shared rules, assets and code are fine.
- An architecture sketch separating the important responsibilities, reflected in the code.
- Practice with at least one useful design pattern: try it on a real problem, compare a simpler alternative, and explain what you kept or changed.
- Actual automated checks, a realistic user journey and a regression check for a meaningful change.
- Real peer feedback, at least one useful change and a retest.
- A short record of what you expected from agent coding, what actually happened, what succeeded or disappointed, and what you would do differently.

[Architecture, patterns and tests](/process/architecture) · [One record to update](/templates/project-record). No extra reflection essay or complete prompt transcript is needed.

## What each review adds

| Review | New progress to show |
| --- | --- |
| **1 · Plan** | Two imaginative concepts; chosen Dream/Promise/Experiment; stack/reason; rough architecture; acceptance checks and one uncertain question. |
| **2 · First working slice** | One new creative journey beyond the completed RPS baseline, or a first useful journey in another app. Core rules separated enough to test; actual success/failure results and a design decision. |
| **3 · Integrated build** | The promise works across its parts. Pattern experiment, A/B choice and regression evidence. Explain whether the uncertain idea worked, changed direction or was dropped. |
| **4 · Improve and hand off** | Real peer issue → meaningful change → retest. Reproducible setup and chosen revision. Explain the architecture, tests and what you learned about agent coding. |

The phases are **Understand → Plan → Build → Improve**. Reviews 2 and 3 both develop Build; a review number is a submission checkpoint, not a different name for a phase.

## The posted baseline is a separate task

[RPS-001](/projects/studios#rps-001-posted) uses the supplied Python starter and its original tests, exclusions and rubric. The creative project's stack freedom applies to the later work; it does not replace that posted assignment. Reuse its code and evidence when useful. If you choose another stack, make a deliberate transition and preserve the completed baseline rather than rewriting it for its original grade.

Future Studios will practice testing/design and architecture/integration in the selected application. Accounts, dashboards or databases belong only where they support your chosen experience and agreed scope.

## Learn from the result

An unexpectedly successful AI attempt deserves investigation too: what made it work, and how did you check it? You do not need to invent a failure. A failed optional experiment can produce valuable learning; a broken working promise still needs repair or an agreed scope change. Feature count, paid tools and fashionable technology do not earn extra credit.
