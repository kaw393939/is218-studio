# Your portfolio: research a direction, show your work

**Build or improve one personal website that helps someone understand you and your work.** Use career research to decide whom it should reach. Show your individual application, your group contribution, and professional materials you can explain.

This is the IS218 portfolio brief. Reuse your existing website if you have one; keep the process and evidence here in IS218. Portfolio review assignments and their rubric are **published in Canvas**: [001 Plan](https://njit.instructure.com/courses/70712/assignments/769206) · [003 Plan](https://njit.instructure.com/courses/70713/assignments/769221) · [all section dates](/course/schedule). Update one portfolio record through the reviews; no additional weekly essay is needed. The current RPS-001 task is unchanged.

## What belongs on the finished site?

- A short introduction: what interests you, what you can demonstrate now, and the opportunity you want next.
- **Individual case study:** working application, its GitHub repository, design/engineering decisions, tests, and honest limits.
- **Group case study:** working application, shared repository, your actual contribution, and evidence of collaboration.
- Your **own GitHub profile**, the portfolio repository, **LinkedIn profile**, and a readable **résumé**.
- A professional contact route and clear navigation on a phone and with a keyboard.

A **case study** is a short project story: problem → your decisions and contribution → checked result → what you learned. Link to the existing specs, issues, PRs and checks instead of copying every development note. Show current group progress until that project is finished; label planned work and update the same case study later.

## Start with a job that interests you

Find one original employer posting. What would you actually do in that job? Write three responsibilities and one skill you want to build. Then use the [career research task](/career/explore) to complete the three-role comparison for your planning hand-in.

Explore [AI Career Atlas](https://kaw393939.github.io/ai-career-research/) and its [research repository](https://github.com/kaw393939/ai-career-research), plus the [regional NY/NJ field guide](https://kaw393939.github.io/is117005-portfolio/). These instructor examples are starting points. Verify original employer sources and investigate work that personally interests you. Your findings become specifications for your own portfolio website and guide accurate updates to LinkedIn and GitHub. [Research examples → your spec](/course/research-examples).

## Use this small design process

Keep one short public portfolio record in the repository with your chosen audience, goals and project evidence. Submit the full role comparison and personal reflection privately in Canvas; do not copy them into the public record. Each step informs the next; no separate report per heading is needed.

### 1. Research three roles and compare two directions

Start with the [IS218 AI-role guide](/career/ai-roles). Find **three original employer descriptions**:

1. One FDE role or internship.
2. One other AI-related technical role with an unfamiliar title or changed responsibilities.
3. One AI-related role in another industry, such as healthcare, finance, manufacturing or media.

For each, save the employer/title, link, date checked, location, eligibility, three actual responsibilities, and a skill gap. Record deadlines as stated; use **not stated** or **not confirmed** when needed. A new-sounding title alone does not show that a job is new. Explain what work is different and what still resembles established software, analysis or implementation work.

Compare **two career directions** using those findings. Choose one main direction for the portfolio and one next learning step. You can be exploring; you do not have to choose your whole career. Senior job descriptions can reveal responsibilities, but should be labeled as future targets rather than immediate application opportunities.

### 2. Choose a reader and a useful promise

A **persona** is a short hypothesis about a reader's situation and needs. Compare two, then choose one primary reader. Avoid invented life stories.

| Reader hypothesis | What they may need to find |
| --- | --- |
| Internship reviewer with limited time | What you built, your contribution, code, checks and résumé. |
| Potential project client | The problem you can help with, a believable example, limits and a contact route. |

Use your role research as evidence; mark assumptions. Write: **“For [reader], this portfolio makes it easy to see [relevant work] and [next action].”** A classmate can check whether the page communicates that promise; they cannot prove how every recruiter will respond.

### 3. Compare two brand and visual directions

Choose three voice words and a brand character. An **archetype** describes that character: a Creator might emphasize original work; a Sage might emphasize clear explanations. It describes the presentation, not a qualification.

Compare two small directions using the **same introduction, projects and main action**. Cite an actual visual reference and name the idea you adapted. Both options still need readable text, clear controls and keyboard access.

Give the reader **honest reasons to trust and act**. Identify two persuasion choices and their limits: a working project with inspectable tests demonstrates relevant ability; a useful explanation offers value before asking for contact. Do not invent testimonials, employment, scarcity, impact metrics or expertise. You are proposing how the design may help, not proving a hiring effect.

### 4. Write a brief spec, then build two small options

Write **three to five user stories** with acceptance checks. A user story connects a reader, a need and a reason.

<details>
<summary>See three portfolio stories and checks</summary>

| Story | Check |
| --- | --- |
| As a reviewer, I want to understand the student's contribution so I can judge the work fairly. | Each case study names personal work, group work, AI/starter help and a limit. |
| As a reviewer, I want to inspect a project so I can see evidence beyond a screenshot. | The demo, repository and relevant check open from the case study. |
| As a reviewer, I want professional materials so I can consider a next conversation. | Résumé, actual GitHub and LinkedIn profiles, and contact are easy to find. |

</details>

Make **two lightweight presentation options** from the same content and components. A second homepage layout or case-study treatment is enough. Your visual drafts can become these options; do not make two extra portfolios or two copies of every project. Choose one and explain the tradeoff.

### 5. Review, improve and finish

Give a real peer this task: **“Find my individual project, tell me what I did in the group project, then find my code, résumé and professional profiles.”** Let them try before explaining.

Record the observation in a GitHub issue in **your portfolio repository**. Make at least one meaningful change, link the revision, repeat the affected task, and record the actual result. A broken link, unclear contribution or hidden résumé matters more than “I like blue.” Use the [review routine](/process/collaboration) and [issue format](/process/github-feedback).

Choose the finished version. Check its links, layout, keyboard access and résumé file. Keep the full career reflection private when it includes personal circumstances; publish suitable goals and project evidence.

## Keep the workload connected

| Point in the work | Update the same portfolio record |
| --- | --- |
| **Research and plan** | Three role cards → two career directions → reader hypothesis → two visual directions → short spec. |
| **Build** | Two small presentation options, current case studies, résumé draft and owned profile/repository links. |
| **Improve and hand off** | Chosen version, real peer issue → change → retest, checked links and updated case studies. |

Use the class pattern: **introduce → next class work/review → submit and carry forward**. Follow the [published section review dates and Canvas links](/course/schedule); this table adds no extra deadlines. Reuse project evidence and the same professional materials rather than producing repeated reports.

**Ready check:** can someone identify your direction, try both projects, distinguish your contribution, inspect code/checks and find your professional materials without coaching?
