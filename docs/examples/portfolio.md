# Two portfolio directions, with feedback

**These are fictional teaching records, not real students, published profiles or completed human reviews.** They show how career research can shape a portfolio. Neither record supplies a live portfolio or actual profile links; the feedback keeps those limits visible.

Use the [portfolio brief](/projects/portfolio) for your work. Verify actual employer responsibilities and eligibility through [AI-role research](/career/ai-roles); the short interests below are student hypotheses, not substitute job research.

**Open one record first.** Both follow the same process; their design choices serve different readers.

## Example A — Sam: customer-facing software work

<details>
<summary>Read Sam's research plan, design comparison and portfolio draft</summary>

### Research → direction → reader

**Three-card research plan:** Palantir's FDE internship; Ellipsis's Senior Backend Engineer, AI Evaluations; Northwell's Senior Product Services Management Analyst in its AI product team. The [role guide](/career/ai-roles) supplies the starting sources. Sam must read and record the actual descriptions, dates, responsibilities and eligibility. The senior roles are future-direction research, not an assertion that Sam qualifies or that an opening is still available.

**Two directions:** customer-facing software delivery versus building/evaluating AI product features. Sam provisionally chooses customer-facing delivery because explaining a workflow and checking its result feels more interesting than focusing only on a model. This preference needs actual role research.

**Reader hypotheses:** an internship reviewer wants evidence of engineering judgment; a small organization's project contact wants a clear example of a useful workflow. Primary reader: internship reviewer.

**Promise:** “See what I built, how I checked it and what I contributed in a few clear steps.”

### Two directions → short spec

**A: Practical builder.** Calm, direct, curious; Creator character. Modernist-inspired grid with project evidence near each claim. **B: Field notebook.** The same content in a more expressive, annotated presentation. Both include the same introduction, projects and contact action. Sam provisionally chooses A for scanability; a real reference and peer comparison are still needed.

Honest persuasion: show inspectable project evidence before claiming competence; offer a useful account of a design mistake and repair. Neither is proof that an employer will hire Sam.

| Story | Acceptance check |
| --- | --- |
| Find an example of my engineering work. | Open the individual demo, repository and actual test result from its card. |
| Understand my role in the team. | The group case study distinguishes my validation work from my partner's dashboard work. |
| Contact me about an appropriate opportunity. | A reader can find a résumé, actual profiles and professional contact route. |

### Portfolio content to build

> **Introduction:** I'm an NJIT student exploring customer-facing software work. I enjoy turning unclear requests into small applications whose behavior I can check and explain.
>
> **Individual case study — RPS exhibit:** The proposed experience lets visitors compare two opponents. The final case study must show the implemented account/game/dashboard journey, tests and a useful architecture decision. Until then, those features remain planned.
>
> **Group case study — equipment requests:** My proposed slice is request validation and error wording; my partner's proposed slice is request history/status. The finished story must replace this allocation with actual work, links and cross-review evidence.
>
> **Next step:** Develop stronger backend and customer-discovery skills while seeking a suitable internship or supervised project.

Keep professional materials consistent: GitHub bio identifies a student learning software delivery; LinkedIn describes actual course work; résumé bullets use only implemented and checked behavior. The final site needs Sam's own links, not invented accounts.

**Review still to do:** ask a real peer to find the two projects, explain Sam's contribution, and locate the résumé/profiles. Record the first obstacle in an issue, change the site and repeat the task. “The résumé link was hard to find” would be a possible observation, not something that happened in this example.

## Example B — Imani: creative systems and evaluation

<details>
<summary>Read Imani's research plan, design comparison and portfolio draft</summary>

### Research → direction → reader

**Three-card research plan:** Palantir's FDE internship; Maki's AI Implementation internship; JPMorgan's Group AI Product Director in financial services. The [role guide](/career/ai-roles) supplies the starting sources. Imani still needs to read the original descriptions and check eligibility/deadlines. The director role is a future-direction comparison, not an immediate application target.

**Two directions:** creative application engineering versus AI workflow evaluation. Imani chooses creative application engineering provisionally and plans to investigate how people evaluate whether an AI feature is useful. The first research set does not yet directly cover creative-tool employment; finding a relevant employer description is a useful next step.

**Reader hypotheses:** a creative-tools internship reviewer wants to inspect an original experience and reliable behavior; a media team wants to understand a useful workflow. Primary reader: creative-tools reviewer.

**Promise:** “Try the experience, then see the rules, experiments and decisions behind it.”

### Two directions → short spec

**A: Playful creator.** Curious, energetic, candid; Creator character. Expressive composition makes experimentation visible. **B: Test bench.** Restrained layout foregrounds comparisons and check results. Both use the same content and actions. Imani prefers A, while keeping a visible route to the technical evidence. The record still needs an actual visual source and user observation to support the choice.

Honest persuasion: the live experience can demonstrate a skill; an explanation of an unsuccessful experiment can help a reader judge the student's decisions. A decorative “AI expert” badge would supply no such evidence.

| Story | Acceptance check |
| --- | --- |
| Try the project before reading its history. | The demo action is clear, with an accessible alternative to animation or gesture controls. |
| Check the student's technical contribution. | Case study links relevant code, meaningful tests and the pattern comparison. |
| See the difference between an experiment and a finished feature. | Each is labeled with actual results, current limits and next steps. |

### Portfolio content to build

> **Introduction:** I'm an NJIT student interested in playful applications and how people judge AI-assisted experiences. I want to build things that feel inventive and behave reliably.
>
> **Individual case study — story duel:** The idea combines simple game rules with a branching narrative. Account, scores and dashboard are planned parts of the application. The portfolio must distinguish tested rules from any uncertain AI narrative experiment.
>
> **Group case study — event content review:** The proposed workflow collects a draft, records review status and shows a clear handoff. Imani must name the implemented slice and actual collaboration instead of claiming the whole application.
>
> **Next step:** Practice evaluating generated output against written criteria and learn more about creative-tool development.

Imani's résumé should describe checked course work rather than imply professional media employment. GitHub, LinkedIn and portfolio claims should agree. A visually inventive site still needs readable text, keyboard operation and an easy-to-find résumé.

**Review still to do:** compare whether a peer can reach the same evidence in A and B without help. Record the actual confusion, make a useful change and retest. Do not call a personal preference a demonstrated usability result.

</details>

## Proposed rubric, provisional scores and feedback

This uses the site's [four-row teaching rubric](/course/milestones#how-the-worked-examples-are-assessed), **25 points per row**. Adoption and any graded portfolio criteria remain planned until posted in Canvas. These are scores for the **supplied draft records**, not final-product grades or evidence of a person's understanding. Missing career sources, sites, owned profiles, execution and real review lower the scores.

| Criterion | Sam | Imani | What is evidenced or missing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Complete · 25** | 13 | 12 | Both identify required contents and a coherent draft; neither provides verified role cards, finished applications, portfolio or owned links. |
| **Thoughtful · 25** | 22 | 21 | Two career/readership/design directions and a bounded spec are visible. Visual sources and an observed A/B decision are still missing. |
| **Checked · 25** | 3 | 3 | Acceptance checks are proposed. No live-site test, real peer issue, executed change or retest is supplied. |
| **Understood · 25** | 20 | 20 | The drafts distinguish interests, plans, individual work and limits. They do not establish a real student's ability to explain implemented code or actual AI results. |
| **Provisional total / 100** | **58** | **56** | A promising plan is the start of the work; it cannot stand in for a finished, checked portfolio. |

**Feedback to Sam:** keep the clear evidence-first structure. First complete the three source-based role cards and use the findings to confirm or revise the direction. Then publish the two lightweight layouts with actual project/profile links. Have a peer attempt the specified task and fix the most important obstacle.

**Feedback to Imani:** keep the imaginative direction and the separation of experiment from reliable behavior. Complete the role cards, add a source closer to the chosen creative-tool direction if needed, name an actual visual reference, and make both layouts usable with a keyboard. Show the project checks and your own group work, then record real feedback and a retest.

**Why the scores are similar:** choosing a playful or restrained style does not earn more points by itself. The difference is whether choices serve the reader and whether the promised experience, claims and review have evidence.
